This handbook collects the practical side of running a Working Group: the spaces and tools GovStack
provides, the help a Working Group can ask for, and the charter template. The rules a Working Group
follows are in the chapter *Working Groups*. Where this handbook and that chapter differ, the
chapter applies.

## Spaces and tools

GovStack provides every Working Group with:

- a public page on the GovStack website;
- a calendar, linked from the Working Group's record in the GovStack Registry;
- a [Slack](http://govstack.slack.com) channel, for immediate communication;
- a member mailing list;
- a [Confluence](https://govstack-global.atlassian.net/wiki/) space, for working documents;
- GitHub repositories in the GovStackWorkingGroup organization, one per publication. A new
  specification starts from `bb-template`.

On request, a Working Group can also have:

- an online meeting platform;
- access to the Sandbox, to test whether new or existing solutions comply with a specification;
- access to GovStack's Swagger, to write and document OpenAPI specifications;
- a [Jira](https://govstack-global.atlassian.net/jira/) project, for tracking tasks.

The facilitators look after these spaces and keep them up to date.

## Help and assistance

A Working Group can ask for help to reach its goals, including:

- **Review and guidance.** The Technical Committee reviews work, answers questions and escalates
  matters to other GovStack teams, during its office hours or directly.
- **Promotion.** GovStack promotes the Working Group's activities and milestones through its
  communication channels and its links with global and regional DPG and DPI networks.
- **Technical mentors.** People from outside the Working Group's area who give a limited number of
  hours to solve a well-defined problem. Facilitators request a mentor through the Technical
  Committee.
- **Funding.** Support from GovStack in applying for funding, or in raising or receiving donations,
  for projects that advance the Working Group's goals.

## Budgeting (experimental)

A Working Group can have a budget for its activities and use it as it sees fit. To request a
budget, the Working Group provides:

- **The project scope.** Every request relates to activities in the Working Group's charter.
- **The budget allocation.** What the budget is for, for example hours for a Lead, a compliance
  evaluation, or the costs of an event.
- **The minutes.** The minutes of the meeting in which the members decided on the request, signed
  by the members who took part.

## Charter template

A Working Group writes its charter from the template in `charter_template.md`. The contents a
charter must have are set in the chapter *Working Groups*, section *The charter*. A charter is
submitted as a file in the `charters/` folder of `govstack-registry`, as described in the
*Creation* steps of that chapter.
